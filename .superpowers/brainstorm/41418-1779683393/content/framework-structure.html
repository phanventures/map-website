A — Framework as Navigation

Habits, Systems, Technology become top-level nav items and page categories. Each gets its own section or page with content, resources, and relevant offerings. The framework IS the site architecture.

Works well if

  • You plan lots of content per pillar (blog, YouTube, courses)
  • SEO is a priority

Watch out for

  • Complex navigation for a personal brand site
  • Dilutes focus on lead capture and conversion

B — Framework as Homepage Story

The three pillars live as one section on the homepage — your methodology, explained visually. Nav stays focused on offerings (Community, Cohort, Consulting). The framework sells the "how," not the navigation.

Works well if

  • Simple nav, conversion-focused homepage
  • Framework clarifies your value prop without fragmenting the site

Watch out for

  • Less SEO surface area per pillar
  • Framework might feel buried as "just a section"

C — Framework as Customer Journey

The three pillars map to how someone progresses through your world: free content builds habits (top of funnel), community builds systems (mid), cohort/consulting integrates tech (high-touch). Each pillar = a stage of the relationship, not a page.

Works well if

  • You want a clear "Start Here" funnel
  • YouTube → free guide → paid is the intended path

Watch out for

  • Less immediately obvious what you offer
  • Requires a strong "Start Here" page to make the journey clear
